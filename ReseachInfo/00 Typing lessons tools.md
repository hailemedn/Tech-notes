# 00 Typing lessons tools

### recommendation: use 2 tools

**1. Keybr — for fixing your actual typing weaknesses**

[Keybr](https://www.keybr.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This is probably the **best fit for you right now**. It adapts practice around the keys you're slow or inaccurate with instead of simply throwing random words at you.

Do **10–15 minutes a day**.

Your goal initially should be something like:

> **40 WPM at 97–98% accuracy**

Don't chase 60 WPM while making lots of mistakes. You're trying to teach your fingers that the correct key is automatic.

---

**2. Monkeytype — for building speed once you've warmed up**

[Monkeytype](https://monkeytype.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

This is where I'd go after Keybr. It's excellent for measuring WPM, accuracy, consistency and individual-character performance.

Try:

- **30-second tests**
- Common English words
- No punctuation/numbers initially
- Don't deliberately type faster than you can control
- Do 5–10 tests

Then occasionally do a **60-second test** to see where you're actually at.

### Your daily routine

I'd literally do this:

**15 minutes total**

**Keybr — 10 min**  
→ Fix weak keys and accuracy.

**Monkeytype — 5 min**  
→ Practice real words and see your WPM.

Do this **5 days a week for a month**.

And here's the important part: **don't look at your WPM every 30 seconds and try to beat your previous score.** That's exactly how you reinforce the "type fast → make mistakes → backspace → type fast" cycle you're describing.

Instead, think:

> **Correct → smooth → fast.**

Your current **30–40 WPM**, with occasional **50–60 WPM**, tells me you probably don't need to learn typing from absolute zero. You need to make your existing typing **more automatic and accurate**.

If you can get yourself to something like **55–60 WPM at 97–99% accuracy**, you'll feel _dramatically_ different when typing emails, Teams messages, passwords, documents, etc. Accuracy is generally more useful than a flashy maximum WPM.

**If you only want ONE website:** pick **Keybr for the next 2–3 weeks**, then make Monkeytype your everyday practice tool.
